= Donate page as a ledger
:description: Show the operating fund, the monthly floor, and the donate button on the same page — instead of a mission sentence and a hop to a third-party form.
:revdate: 2026-08-11

Most donation pages hide the books. A visitor sees a mission sentence and a button. They do not see whether last month’s costs are covered, whether the org is sitting on unused cash, or what “enough” even means.

A better ask treats the donate page as a **ledger**: current balance, the minimum required to keep the service running, an as-of date, and the work that money pays for — then the button.

== The pattern (Transparency HOA)

link:https://www.transparencyhoa.org/analyze-queue[Transparency HOA’s Analyze Queue] (a 501(c)(3) founded in 2019) puts the ask on the same page as the work queue.

As captured in 2026, the page states that the Analyze service costs about **$6,000 per month**, shows an **Analysis Fund** balance (**$7,881** as of 30 April 2026), and keeps **Donate Now** on that same view. Below the fund, a pending list names the associations waiting for analysis.

Screenshot of the public Analyze Queue page. Used here as a cited example of an on-site fund ask. Source: link:https://www.transparencyhoa.org/analyze-queue[transparencyhoa.org/analyze-queue].

The numbers will change. The design lesson does not: **cost + balance + date + ask + work**, on one page, under the org’s own brand.

== What to put on the page

* **Floor** — the minimum to stay in operation for the period you actually run (monthly burn for a service, quarterly for a slower program). Say what the number includes.
* **Balance or raised** — cash on hand for that fund, or amount raised toward a campaign. Label which it is.
* **As-of date** — stale totals destroy trust faster than no totals.
* **Ask** — donate (or sponsor) in the same view. The payment processor can still be Stripe, Open Collective, or a fiscal host; do not exile the *story* to a blank third-party form.
* **What the money buys** — a queue, a milestone list, a public budget line. Visitors should see work, not only a thermometer.

A goal amount is useful when you are filling a campaign. A **floor** is more honest when the real question is “can this keep running next month?” You can show both: floor as the line you must not fall under, goal as the next useful surplus.

== Why this beats a generic donate button

* **Trust** — published numbers beat “please support our mission.”
* **Urgency without theater** — a balance near the floor is a reason to give; a surplus is also honest (the example above was *above* one month of burn).
* **Ownership** — the ask stays on your site. Hosted fundraisers are fine as the checkout rail, not as the only page a donor ever sees.

This works for 501(c) organizations **and** for donation-supported products or OSS projects. The legal wrapper changes the tax receipt; it does not change the UX.

== What not to fake

* Do not invent a burn rate. If you do not know monthly cost, publish a smaller true number (hosting only) rather than a rounded “we need $X.”
* Do not hide a large reserve behind a crisis headline.
* Do not copy another org’s dollar figures into your own page.
* Tax deductibility, fiscal-host fees, and charitable status are jurisdiction-specific. This guide is a page pattern, not legal or accounting advice.
